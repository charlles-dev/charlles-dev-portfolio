# Page quality bar

## Structure

- The page's purpose is obvious within the first viewport.
- One primary action has visual priority; secondary actions remain available without competing.
- Sections change rhythm or composition because their content changes, not merely to create variety.
- Long text stays within a readable measure; metadata is scannable.
- Mobile preserves hierarchy and touch reachability without horizontal overflow.

## Content

- Headings name a concrete idea; descriptions add new information.
- Each project claim is traceable to code, a public link, or clearly labeled intent.
- Dates and “current” statements expose freshness rather than appearing evergreen by accident.
- Calls to action describe the outcome or destination.
- Locales preserve meaning and structure, not word-for-word awkwardness.

## Interaction

- Focus is visible and never hidden behind fixed chrome.
- Dialogs have an accessible name, initial focus, a focus trap, Escape close, and focus restoration.
- Icon-only controls have accessible names and at least a 44 by 44 CSS-pixel target.
- Loading, empty, failure, disabled, success, and copied states exist where relevant.
- External links are distinguishable and safe; hashes and browser history remain shareable.

## Visual system

- Color, type, spacing, radius, border, and motion values resolve to shared tokens.
- Green highlights decisions and active state; it does not become wallpaper.
- Depth comes mainly from contrast, overlap, cropping, and restrained borders, not card stacks.
- The hero owns the cinematic spectacle. Supporting routes favor editorial clarity.
